# Express Checkout Concept Test

Study: One-Tap Express Checkout Concept Test
Date: 2026-05-06
Method: Unmoderated concept test with prototype
Participants: P69, P70, P71, P72, P73, P74, P75, P76

Participant P69:
"One tap and it's bought? Yes please. I buy the same coffee pods every month, I don't need five screens for that."

Participant P70:
"That scares me a bit. My kids play on my phone. What stops them from ordering something by accident?"

Participant P71:
"I'd want to see the total with shipping before it goes through. One tap is fine as long as the price I see is the price I pay."

Participant P72:
"It's fast, but I like the review screen. It's my last chance to notice I picked the wrong colour."

Researcher observation:
Reactions split along purchase type. Participants buying repeat, low-cost items welcomed one-tap buying; participants buying new or expensive items wanted a review step.

Participant P73:
"If there was an undo for like a minute after, I'd be totally fine with it."

Participant P74:
"Honestly this is how Amazon works and I use it all the time. Anything slower feels broken now."

Participant P75:
"I don't trust it with a new shop. Maybe after I've ordered a few times and nothing went wrong."

Participant P76:
"Where's it sending it? It didn't show me the address. That's the bit I'd want to check."

Researcher observation:
Five of eight participants said they would use express checkout, but most attached a condition: a visible total, a visible delivery address, or an undo window. Accidental purchases were the main fear.
