# AI Shopping Assistant Concept

Study: AI Shopping Assistant Concept Test
Date: 2026-06-23
Method: Moderated concept test with interactive prototype
Participants: P154, P155, P156, P157, P158, P159, P160

Participant P154:
"I asked it for a present for a ten year old who likes space and it gave me three good ideas. Faster than browsing."

Participant P155:
"How do I know it's not just pushing whatever the shop wants to sell? Is it sponsored?"

Participant P156:
"It told me a jacket was waterproof and the product page said water resistant. Now I don't trust anything it says."

Researcher observation:
Participants valued the assistant for open-ended gift and discovery tasks, but a single factual error sharply reduced trust. Participants wanted to know whether recommendations were sponsored.

Participant P157:
"I'd rather just use filters. Typing a whole sentence is slower for me."

Participant P158:
"I liked that it linked to the actual reviews when it said something was comfortable."

Participant P159:
"It's a bit creepy that it knew what I bought last month."

Participant P160:
"For comparing two blenders it was brilliant. It pulled out the differences that mattered."

Researcher observation:
Answers that cited product pages or reviews were trusted more. Comparison and gift-finding were the strongest use cases; routine searches were faster with filters.
